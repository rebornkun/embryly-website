Pumpkin Spice Lattes During Pregnancy: Check Your Cup

Check your pumpkin spice latte's size, caffeine and ingredients during pregnancy using current information for the actual drink.

Embryly Editorial Team
Editorial date: October 11, 2026 · Published October 14, 2026 · Sources checked October 14, 2026

The season arrives in a cup, and the cup arrives in several sizes. For a pumpkin spice latte during pregnancy, check the actual caffeine amount, ingredients and serving size rather than using the drink name as the answer. ACOG advises keeping daily caffeine below 200 milligrams. Its coffee guidance supplies the general US recommendation.

Check this year's actual recipe

A small drink, a large drink and one with extra espresso shots can contain different amounts. Use the cafe's current official information for the exact size and version. Do not rely on last autumn's screenshot or a number for another country's menu.

If the amount is not clear, ask the cafe or choose a version whose caffeine information is available. A decaffeinated option can make planning easier, but decaffeinated does not necessarily mean absolutely caffeine free.

Count the rest of the day

Your morning coffee, later tea and other caffeine sources belong in the same total. Suppose one product lists 90 milligrams and another lists 70: together they contribute 160. Those are illustrative numbers, not amounts for a particular latte. The product information is what makes the calculation meaningful.

Choosing a smaller size or fewer shots changes the drink you actually receive. Asking for less syrup may change sweetness without changing the espresso amount. Keep those adjustments separate rather than assuming every customization reduces caffeine.

Look at the milk and toppings

Use appropriately pasteurized dairy, and check ingredients for allergies or dietary needs. Plant drinks differ in composition and fortification, and seasonal sauces may include ingredients not obvious from the milk choice. Ask about the specific version, including any topping.

The FDA dairy guide provides preparation context. A cafe may use a commercial sauce rather than fresh pumpkin or a homemade custard. The label or official recipe is more useful than guessing from the color of the foam.

Keep nutrition separate from guilt

A sweet drink can be part of the food conversation without becoming a measure of your character. If your care team has given you a specific eating plan, follow it. ACOG nutrition guidance describes a broader eating pattern; a blog should not turn one seasonal order into either a health claim or a scolding.

There is also no need to declare every ordinary culinary spice a special pregnancy hazard without evidence. Unfamiliar supplement doses or concentrated products are different questions and belong with your clinician or pharmacist.

A useful order is a specific order

Choose the size, shots, milk and topping that fit what you have verified. Embryly can help with general ingredient questions or packaged product information, but a photograph of your drink cannot measure caffeine. Check the details, count the total and enjoy the autumn ritual with less guesswork.

General educational guidance based on US sources. Your care team can advise on individual circumstances. Artwork is illustrative.
