The important word on the can may be smaller than the brand name. **Pregnancy tuna guidance depends on the species and amount, not just whether the label says tuna.** Canned light tuna, albacore, yellowfin and bigeye do not occupy the same category on the [FDA and EPA fish chart](source:fish).

## Read past “in water” or “in oil”

Those phrases describe the packing medium, not the mercury category. Look for the fish type. Canned light tuna, including skipjack, is listed among Best Choices; albacore or white tuna and yellowfin are Good Choices; bigeye is a Choice to Avoid.

A pouch can contain a different tuna type from a can that looks similar. Check the product every time you change versions. If the package is vague, ask the manufacturer or choose a product with clearer species information.

## The serving guidance is an either-or plan

For pregnancy, the FDA chart recommends two to three servings a week from Best Choices, or one serving from Good Choices. An adult serving in this guidance is four ounces. Do not add the Good Choices serving on top of a full Best Choices week as though the lists were separate allowances. The [FDA questions and answers](source:fishqa) explain how to apply the chart.

Think about all the fish you eat that week, not just tuna. A can at lunch, a fish dinner and another seafood meal belong in the same planning conversation. You do not need an exact weekly menu to notice which category each choice uses.

## Canned and raw are separate decisions

A tuna steak or sushi filling also raises a cooking question. Choosing a species with a lower mercury category does not make raw fish appropriate under US pregnancy guidance. Likewise, cooking a higher mercury species does not remove the mercury.

For a restaurant order, ask which tuna is used and how it is cooked. “Ahi” can be an imprecise menu term; get the species when the answer matters. If that information is unavailable, another clearly identified seafood option may be easier to choose.

## A tuna lunch needs normal handling

Once you open a can or mix a salad, follow appropriate refrigeration and storage instructions. The shelf life of the sealed can does not become the shelf life of your prepared lunch. Keep cold food cold during transport, and use clean utensils.

Tuna is not the only practical protein for lunch. Beans, thoroughly cooked poultry and other foods that fit your needs can provide variety. Choose them because they work for your day, not because every meal has to solve the same nutrient question.
