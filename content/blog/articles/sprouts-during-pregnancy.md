The little tangle on top of a sandwich can be easy to overlook. **US pregnancy guidance recommends avoiding raw or undercooked sprouts and choosing sprouts cooked thoroughly instead.** Washing is not an equivalent workaround. The [FDA produce guidance](source:produce) explains why sprouts need a different approach from many other vegetables.

## Why sprouts get a separate rule

Sprouting conditions help a seed grow, but they can also help harmful germs multiply. Contamination can occur where a rinse will not reliably remove it. The problem is not simply a bit of visible dirt on the outside.

That is why buying organic, growing them at home or giving them a longer wash does not replace the cooking recommendation. A jar of home-grown sprouts has the same preparation question as a supermarket container. The [CDC pregnancy food table](source:cdc) includes raw and undercooked sprouts among riskier choices.

## Know where to look

Sprouts can appear in sandwiches, salads, wraps, noodle dishes and garnishes. Alfalfa and bean sprouts are familiar examples, but do not assume only one kind is relevant. Check the actual ingredient rather than guessing from a small picture on the menu.

When ordering, a simple request works: “Please leave out any raw sprouts.” For a cooked dish, ask that the sprouts be thoroughly cooked rather than added raw at the end. [FDA restaurant guidance](source:restaurant) specifically recommends asking for raw sprouts to be left out.

## A quick toss is not necessarily thorough cooking

If preparing a stir fry, include sprouts in the cooking step and follow an appropriate recipe. Sprinkling them over a finished bowl and mixing once is a different process. Choose a method that heats them fully rather than preserving a raw garnish for appearance.

Keep raw produce and its utensils from contaminating food that is already ready to eat. Wash hands and clean surfaces during preparation. Cooking the sprouts does not make every later handling step irrelevant.

## You can keep the crunch another way

Washed cucumber, shredded cabbage or another vegetable that fits your meal can provide texture. Choose based on the recipe and your preferences, and handle those ingredients appropriately too. There is no need to redesign the whole sandwich because one garnish changes.

Microgreens and sprouts are not automatically interchangeable names. If a label is unclear, ask what the product actually is rather than treating a social-media explanation as its identification. Use current official guidance for the item you have.

## Let the scan lead to a better question

Embryly can help identify possible ingredients, but a small garnish may be missed or mistaken for something else. Look at the meal, ask the preparer and mention relevant details when seeking guidance. A useful result is “I should check whether those are raw sprouts,” not a promise that every green item in the picture was recognized.
