Shrimp is often the easier seafood question on a crowded menu. **It is a lower mercury choice in FDA and EPA guidance, and pregnancy food guidance recommends eating it thoroughly cooked.** Raw shrimp, partially cooked shrimp and a properly cooked dish are not interchangeable. Check the [fish chart](source:fish) and [seafood preparation guidance](source:meat).

## Check what “cooked” means in this dish

Grilled, boiled or sauteed shrimp can be suitable preparations when cooked appropriately. A ceviche dish may use acid rather than a cooking step. Lemon or lime juice changes texture, but it does not replace thorough cooking for pregnancy food safety.

Ask about the actual preparation instead of relying on the restaurant category. A taco might contain fully cooked shrimp and uncooked toppings. A sushi roll might add raw seafood on top of cooked shrimp. The ingredient you recognize is not necessarily the only ingredient that matters.

## Cook the shrimp, then keep the utensils clean

The [official temperature and doneness chart](source:temps) says shrimp should be cooked until the flesh is pearly or white and opaque. Follow product and recipe instructions, particularly for frozen shrimp or mixed dishes. Avoid using the spoon or plate that held raw seafood for the finished meal unless it has been cleaned.

If adding shrimp to soup or a skillet, make sure the seafood itself cooks properly. A hot sauce poured over raw shrimp is not proof that the shrimp reached the appropriate doneness. A large crowded pan can also heat unevenly, so use a method that allows all pieces to cook.

## Cold shrimp needs cold handling

A cooked shrimp appetizer is still perishable. Ask how it has been held if it has been sitting on a buffet, and keep a homemade dish appropriately chilled. “It was cooked earlier” answers only the cooking question, not everything that happened afterward.

For takeaway, plan to eat promptly or refrigerate appropriately. Do not let a seafood box sit in a warm car. At home, thaw and store according to official instructions rather than leaving a bag on the counter for the day.

## Keep the plate practical

Shrimp can go with rice and cooked vegetables, pasta or another meal format you enjoy. Rotate foods across the week rather than treating one ingredient as a required daily fix. If shellfish does not fit your allergies or preferences, choose another protein and ask your care team about individual nutrition needs.

## What a scan can add

Embryly may help identify shrimp or other ingredients in a dish and explain general considerations. It cannot establish doneness, storage history or allergen cross contact from the photo. Use the result to ask a specific question about the preparation. A short conversation with the cook is more useful than a confident guess based on the color of a picture.
