The short version

Embryly combines public reference guidance, prepared meal records, matching rules, and AI generated information. Source citations are not medical approval. No independently validated overall accuracy percentage is established.

1. What accuracy means here

Validated overall accuracy: not established. No percentage is available. We have not identified an independent evaluation supporting a single accuracy percentage across food identification, ingredient detection, nutrient estimation, pregnancy suitability, and baby artwork. Those are different tasks and would require different tests. A confidence score is not the chance that a meal is safe for a particular person.

The existing app contains legacy “up to 90% accuracy” wording. This preview does not repeat it as a performance claim. It should be removed or supported by a clearly scoped, reproducible evaluation before use. No clinical approval or kitchen testing is implied by an automated editorial check.

2. The references in our catalog

These public resources appear in the app’s catalog source records or editorial publication notes. They supply background guidance or reference data; the organizations have not endorsed Embryly.

  • CDC: Safer Food Choices for Pregnant Women — general food selection and handling guidance.
  • FoodSafety.gov: People at Risk, Pregnant Women — pregnancy food safety considerations.
  • FoodSafety.gov: Safe Minimum Internal Temperatures — cooking and reheating reference temperatures.
  • FDA/EPA: Advice About Eating Fish — fish choices and frequency guidance.
  • NHS: Have a healthy diet in pregnancy — general dietary variety guidance.
  • USDA FoodData Central and SR Legacy data — reference ingredient nutrients. AI selected ingredient matches and recipe sums introduce uncertainty.
  • Open Food Facts — community maintained barcode, ingredient, allergen, and nutrition records. This is product data, not clinical advice.

Links checked September 26, 2026. Country specific advice can differ. Check the original source and your local care team rather than assuming every rule is universal.

3. How content reaches your screen

The starting catalog combines prepared meal records, generated imagery, ingredient tags, recipes, and estimated nutrient data. The current publication records describe an automated editorial review, not independent medical review. The selection rules filter and rank matching records using pregnancy week, saved preferences, and recent meal history.

Food scans use an AI model and, for barcodes, product lookup data. They are not guaranteed to consult these reference pages during each response. Nutrition estimates are not laboratory measurements. Recommendations do not establish a complete daily nutrient intake or a prescribed diet.

4. About the baby and meal visuals

Baby images for weeks 1 through 42 are artistic, AI assisted illustrations. They are not clinical reference plates, personalized scans, or verified depictions of exact development in a particular week. They cannot date a pregnancy or assess a baby’s growth, anatomy, viability, or wellbeing. A clinician’s measurements and examinations are the appropriate basis for those questions.

Meal imagery is illustrative and can differ from actual ingredients, portions, or cooking results. Marketing screenshots display real app screens with fictional sample records. They are product demonstrations, not patient outcomes. No visual is evidence that a particular food is safe.

5. Questions and corrections

If a meal, image, source, or scan appears wrong, report it to us. Describe the content and the correction you believe is needed without sending unnecessary personal health information. Check the original label or source and seek a professional opinion if the issue affects a decision about your health.

For personal medical questions or suspected illness, contact your care team. Our support channel is for product and content issues, not clinical assessment.
